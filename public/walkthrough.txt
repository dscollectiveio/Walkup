WALKUP — HOW TO USE IT
From signing in for the first time to running the building every month.
==========================================================================

Walkup keeps a small condo association's books, dues, taxes, insurance and
repairs in one place. It reads your bank (read-only), but it never moves
money and never sends anything on its own — every payment and every email
still goes out from you.

Keyboard: press [ to shrink the sidebar to icons, ] to bring it back.
Help: the Help button at the top right of every page has the short version.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 1 — GETTING IN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Create your account
   - Go to the sign-in page and choose "Create an account".
   - Enter your email and a password (twice), tick the privacy notice, and
     submit.
   - If a board member invited you, open the invite link from their email
     instead — it brings you straight to their building after you sign up.

2. Turn on two-step sign-in (required)
   - Walkup holds your association's financial records, so every account
     uses an authenticator app (Google Authenticator, 1Password, Authy…).
   - Scan the QR code it shows you, then type the 6-digit code to confirm.
   - From then on, each sign-in is: email + password, then the current
     6-digit code from the app.

3. Create your building (first board member only)
   - Enter your full name, the association's legal name (as on the
     declaration), the short name you'll see in the app (usually the
     address), and the state.
   - Walkup sets up the fiscal year, the operating and reserve funds, and a
     chart of accounts for you. You land on the Dashboard.

   Invited members skip this step — they join the building that invited
   them.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 2 — SETTING UP (about an hour, can be spread over a week)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Dashboard shows "Set up your building": a checklist in the right order. Each
step ticks itself off when the information exists — there is nothing to
check off by hand. You can skip it and come back; it stays on the Dashboard until
it's done.

STEP 1 — BUILDING & OWNERS  (Sidebar: Building & Owners)
   a. Fill in the association's details: EIN, city, county, date
      incorporated. The EIN is needed for the tax return; the date
      incorporated is what the Illinois annual-report reminder is worked
      out from.
   b. Add every unit (e.g. Unit 1, Unit 2, Garden).
   c. Assign an owner to each unit — name and email. The email is what
      reminders and invites use.
   d. Add the rest of the board and give each person their role.
   e. Invite them to sign in. They get a link; nothing is emailed by
      Walkup itself.

STEP 2 — FINANCES & DUES
   a. Connect the bank  (Bank Feed & Transactions)
      - Click "Connect your bank" and sign in to your bank through Plaid.
        Walkup gets read-only access: it can see transactions and
        balances, never move money.
      - If the association has money at a second bank (often the reserve),
        use "+ Add another bank" and tell Walkup which account it is.
   b. Sync transactions — "Sync now" pulls in recent history.
   c. Categorize and post the backlog — see "The monthly routine" below.
      The first time this may take 20–30 minutes; after that, ten.
   d. Financial statements fill themselves in once transactions are
      posted. Nothing to set up.
   e. Set this year's budget  (Budget & spending)
      - "Edit budget" → one number per category for the year.
      - If you have last year's records, Walkup offers them as a starting
        point (with an optional +2–5% for rising costs).
      - Plan a yearly amount to set aside for the reserve, too.
   f. Tell owners how to pay  (HOA Dues → How to pay)
      - Payee name, bank, account and routing numbers, Zelle — whatever
        owners should use. Owners see this on their own Dues page.
   g. Set each unit's dues  (HOA Dues → Dues plan, far right of each row)
      - Enter what each unit pays for the year and how often (monthly,
        quarterly, once a year). Units can all be different.
      - Optional: a late-fee rule (amount and grace days). It's off until
        you set it, and even then fees are only ever proposed — you click
        to add each one.

STEP 3 — TAXES & INSURANCE
   a. EIN on file (from Step 1a).
   b. Taxes  (Taxes)
      - Classify your income once. Each income account with activity gets
        one question — owner fees, or not (interest, laundry, parking…).
        Walkup pre-fills the answer; you confirm it. The 1120-H tests
        wait until every account is confirmed.
      - Add your accountant: name, firm, email. Every email draft Walkup
        prepares is addressed to them. "Give them read access to the
        books" is a separate link, with an end date.
      - Answer "How do you file 1099s with the IRS?" — on paper,
        electronically (IRIS), or not sure yet. Form 1096 only goes with
        paper 1099s.
      - Illinois buildings: add the corporation file number — the
        Secretary of State's annual-report portal asks for it.
      - "Add these dates to your calendar" downloads every due date, with
        reminders, for Apple Calendar, Google Calendar or Outlook. "Send
        the dates to the board" opens an email draft with the list.
      At year end, Taxes works out the 1120-H from your posted books and
      shows you what to file, when, and from which official form.
   c. Record the insurance policy  (Insurance Marketplace)
      - Carrier, policy number, dates, premium, deductible, limits.
      - Upload the declarations page if you have it.
      - The premium is added to your recurring bills automatically.

STEP 4 — BUILDING UPKEEP  (Repairs & Upkeep)
   a. Add your contractors  (Contractors view) — the plumber, electrician,
      roofer, cleaners you already use. If Google search is turned on,
      "Find a contractor" searches near the building.
   b. Collect W-9s from each contractor you pay — you need them before you
      can issue 1099s in January. Record "W-9 on file" on their profile.
      Taxes has an "Ask for a W-9" email draft for each one still missing.
   c. Set up the upkeep schedule  (Upkeep view)
      - "Start with the usual checklist" offers the dozen jobs most
        buildings do every year (boiler service, gutters, backflow test,
        sewer rodding…). Untick what doesn't apply.
   d. Upload the declaration and the insurance certificate  (Document Hub).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 3 — THE MONTHLY ROUTINE (about 15 minutes)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Bank Feed & Transactions
   - The page opens on what needs a category, with a "to go" count and a
     progress bar.
   - Each transaction shows a RECOMMENDED category with the reason
     ("you filed this payee the same way 4 times before"). One click files
     it and posts it to the books. A filled chip is how you filed that
     payee before; an outlined one is a guess. "+3 more" means the other
     transactions from the same payee go with it.
   - "Save for later" beside the chip files it without posting; "Post N
     saved" at the top posts everything you saved.
   - No recommendation, or it's wrong? Click the row (or "Categorize") and
     start typing — Utilities, Dues, Transfer, Not for the books — then
     pick from the list and Save & post.
   - Many at once: "Post all N recommended" posts what your own history
     backs; or tick the boxes and a bar appears at the bottom of the
     screen to file them together.
   - Made a mistake? Every post shows Undo for 10 seconds, and a posted
     row always has "Undo posting". Posting is what updates the budget,
     dues balances and statements.

2. HOA Dues
   - "Needs you" lists everything waiting, one decision per line:
     * "October dues haven't been charged yet" → Charge what's due.
     * "$450 came in on Oct 3 — looks like Unit 2" → Yes, Unit 2.
     * "Unit 3 says they paid by Zelle" → it's matched automatically when
       the deposit shows up; otherwise Keep waiting or Not received.
     * A late fee, if you set a rule → Add the fee, or Skip this time.
     * "Unit 1 is behind" → Draft a reminder (opens in your own email).
   - A check or cash handed to you: "Record a payment".

3. Budget & spending
   - One sentence at the top says whether you're on plan.
   - Each category shows spent vs. planned, with a thin line for where
     you'd be if spending were spread evenly. Over-budget categories are
     listed first. Click one to see what's in it.

4. Dashboard
   - "Worth a minute today" lists anything that needs attention (late
     dues, a category over budget, an insurance renewal coming up, a tax
     form due soon, missing W-9s). It empties itself as things get
     handled.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 4 — AS THINGS COME UP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Something breaks
   Repairs & Upkeep → Problems → "Report a problem". Anyone in the building
   can report one. The board marks it urgent if needed, assigns a
   contractor, and closes it when it's fixed.

Upkeep comes due
   Repairs & Upkeep → Upkeep. Overdue jobs are at the top. When a job is
   done, "Mark done" with the date, cost and who did it — the next date
   rolls forward on its own.

Board meeting or an owner asks for the numbers
   Financial Statements → pick the month, quarter or year → Download PDF.
   The "···" menu has Excel, an email draft, and the year-end package for
   your accountant.

Insurance renewal
   The Dashboard warns you 60 days ahead. Insurance Marketplace has the policy, and
   lets you compare quotes year to year.

Year end
   1. Make sure every transaction for the year is posted.
   2. Taxes → "This year" says what is due next and the one thing to do
      about it. Each form card shows why you need it, the official IRS or
      Illinois link, and the due date — with whether a person has checked
      that date or it is estimated.
   3. Open the 1120-H form → "Send to your accountant": download the
      figures worksheet (and the editable PDF, once Walkup has that
      year's official form checked), open the email draft, attach the
      files and send it from your own email, then "Mark as sent". Have an
      accountant look before you file — Walkup is not a tax adviser.
   4. 1099-NECs: Taxes lists every contractor paid at or above the IRS
      threshold for that year's payments, shows the amount it used and
      whether a person has checked it (the threshold changed for payments
      made from 2026). W-9s first. Walkup prepares the contractor's copies
      only — file with the IRS through IRIS or on official forms, never a
      Copy A printed from the IRS website.
   5. Once a form is filed, "Mark as filed" on its page records the date
      and, for the 1120-H, locks that year's figures.
   6. Budget & spending → "Start from the last budget" for next year.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
FOR OWNERS (not on the board)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

   - HOA Dues shows what you owe, what each charge is for, and how to pay.
   - Paid by Zelle, check or cash? Click "I paid another way" so the board
     can match it when the deposit lands.
   - Something broken in a shared area or your unit? Report it under
     Repairs & Upkeep.
   - Your unit's full statement is linked from the Dues page.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
FOR THE WALKUP ADMIN (one-time setup per form and year — not a board job)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

   - Open /admin/tax-templates ("Official tax forms, links and rules").
   - "Set up from Walkup's suggestions": one click per form fetches the
     official PDF from irs.gov and installs Walkup's field map for it —
     only if the file is the exact revision the map was written for.
     Otherwise it is stored inactive, with a note and no map.
   - Open the labeled preview and the filled test export, check every
     mapped line, the due rule and the links, then "Verify and activate".
     Nothing fills from a form until a person has done this.
   - "Official links": open each one and click "I opened this and it's
     current". "Rules and thresholds": "Mark checked today" once a rule
     is confirmed against the instructions. Both read "not yet checked by
     a person" to every board until then.
   - Each January the page asks you to check the active forms for
     updates. A changed file becomes a new, inactive template to check —
     never an automatic switch.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
GOOD TO KNOW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

   - Nothing moves money. Owners pay from their own bank; you pay bills
     from yours. Walkup records and reminds.
   - Nothing is sent automatically. Reminder emails open as drafts in your
     own email app.
   - Posted entries are never deleted. "Undo posting" writes a reversing
     entry, so the history always adds up.
   - Walkup fills an official tax form only after a person has opened
     that year's form and checked every mapped line — a one-time setup
     per form and year, done by Walkup's admin, not the board. Until
     then you still get your figures, the official link and the email
     draft to your accountant.
   - A tax date, threshold or link marked "estimated" or "not yet
     checked" is exactly that: retrieved from the IRS or Illinois, not
     yet confirmed by a person. Ask your CPA before relying on it.
   - Light or dark: the moon/sun button at the bottom of the sidebar.
